# RFC Draft Checklist

## Structure

- does the document have one clear problem statement
- does each major section have one main job
- are examples separated from the normative or conceptual core
- are open questions explicitly marked instead of mixed into settled text

## Prose

- is the language clear and concise
- are double negatives avoided
- are abbreviations expanded on first use
- is terminology used consistently
- is inclusive language preserved

## References And Links

- are HTTPS links used where possible
- is citation style consistent throughout the draft
- are links useful rather than repetitive
- do link texts make the destination obvious

## RFC Editor Alignment

- is the title specific enough to orient a new reader
- is the chosen category consistent with the draft's intent
- is boilerplate work deferred until the document shape is stable
- are sections short enough to stay referenceable and editable

## Continuity Discipline

- does the draft still match the current local continuity node
- has the draft avoided inventing a second primary source of truth
- are origin, parent, references, and integrity kept conceptually separate
- if the draft diverges from the lineage, is that divergence stated plainly

## Local Steering

- if `policy.md` exists, is the draft consistent with it
- if `policy.md` does not exist, has the draft avoided inventing heavy local policy anyway
- does `process.md` read as operational guidance rather than as a second policy layer
- are support files still reference surfaces rather than competing primaries
