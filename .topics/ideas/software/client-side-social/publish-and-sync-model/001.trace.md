# Publish And Sync Model

This topic narrows the client-side social product into one of its most critical
surfaces: how something gets written locally, published outward, synchronized,
and exported without losing user ownership.

## Current Read

The strongest product distinction here is that publishing and syncing should not
be the same thing.

Local authorship should remain primary.

Network propagation should be an explicit later step or optional background
capability, not the hidden precondition for the product to function.

## Design Direction

Good continuation in this line should usually prefer:

- local-first writes
- explicit publish actions
- optional or configurable sync behavior
- exportability that does not depend on a platform staying alive
- delaying exact protocol and trust-layer decisions to later children

## Likely Receiver Humans

Strong downstream artifacts here should usually stay useful for:

- product designers shaping user control
- protocol-minded engineers
- privacy-conscious operators
- teams defining how user-owned continuity meets social distribution

## Good Child Candidates

- draft versus published state model
- manual versus automatic sync policies
- multi-home or multi-lineage publishing
- conflict handling and merge semantics
- export and backup after publication

## Next Artifacts

- continue narrower children when one state-transition or sync-policy slice is
  clear enough to deserve deeper design work
- attach implementation grounding later when this line needs stronger protocol
  or storage evidence
