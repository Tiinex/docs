# In-House Hosting

This topic narrows the business-IT branch into an operational line for
organizations that want to host meaningful parts of their service stack
themselves.

## Current Read

The point of this line is not only cost control.

The point is operational ownership.

That includes cases where an ISP-like or mixed internal hosting team wants to
run services from its own racks, rooms, cages, or partner facilities while
keeping more direct control over data placement, failure handling, and customer
service quality.

## Design Direction

Good continuation in this line should usually prefer:

- service ownership over abstract platform fashion
- mixed real-world hosting models rather than false cloud-versus-on-prem purity
- continuity of operations, backups, migrations, and customer handling as first
  class concerns
- leaving exact hardware, virtualization, and network architecture decisions to
  later children

## Likely Receiver Humans

Strong downstream artifacts here should usually stay legible for:

- infrastructure managers
- hosting operators
- internal IT owners
- managed service leads
- small ISP or colo-adjacent teams

## Good Child Candidates

- managed tenant hosting
- internal service platforms
- migration from vendor cloud to owned hosting
- mixed colo and on-site operations
- customer-facing continuity and SLA design

## Next Artifacts

- continue narrower children when one operating model becomes concrete enough to
  deserve its own lineage
- attach software, storage, or network grounding later when a child depends on
  real implementation evidence
